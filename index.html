Concentric Hypersphere Embedding for Pairwise Similarity Learning

An angular score tells how alike two faces look, not how much evidence supports it. RipPLE keeps the norm: direction encodes identity, the norm carries evidence, and one constraint on the mean norm keeps the loss temperature in a workable range.

Method

One embedding, two quantities

Standard practice fixes every norm to one, discarding how informative each image is. RipPLE lets the pairwise loss decide it instead.

01

Direction is identity

The unit vector still separates identities, so the geometry of the recognition problem is unchanged.

02

Norm is evidence

A clear face grows a long vector, an occluded or blurred one shrinks. An uncertain pair then scores near zero instead of looking like a confident impostor.

03

ENC anchors the mean

Only the batch mean is anchored; each individual norm stays free. This is what separates RipPLE from a fixed-norm baseline.
