Concentric Hypersphere Embedding for Pairwise Similarity Learning

Face embeddings are usually normalized onto a hypersphere, so an angular score says how alike two faces look but not how much evidence supports it. RipPLE keeps the norm: the direction encodes identity, the norm carries evidence, and a single constraint on the mean norm keeps training stable.

One embedding, two quantities

Standard practice fixes every norm to one, which discards how informative each image is. RipPLE lets the pairwise loss decide it, while a single anchor keeps the loss temperature in a workable range.

Direction — identity

The unit vector still does the discriminating, so the geometry of the recognition problem is unchanged.

Norm — evidence

A clear face grows a long vector, an occluded or blurred one shrinks. The score follows, so an uncertain pair scores near zero instead of looking like a confident impostor.

ENC — one anchor

Only the mean norm is anchored per batch; every individual norm stays free, which is what separates RipPLE from a fixed-norm baseline.

Watch the evidence disappear

Both faces of the genuine pair are blurred together, the impostor reference stays sharp. The scores are traced point by point as the blur grows, and at the heaviest blur the two cross.
