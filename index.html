Concentric Hypersphere Embedding for Pairwise Similarity Learning

An angular score says how alike two faces look, not how much evidence supports it. RipPLE keeps the norm: direction is identity, length is evidence.

One embedding, two quantities

Normalised embeddings throw away how informative an image is. RipPLE lets the pairwise loss decide it, and anchors only the collective scale so training stays stable.

Direction — identity
The unit vector still separates identities, so the geometry of the recognition problem is unchanged.
Norm — evidence
A clear face grows a long vector, an occluded or blurred one shrinks. An uncertain pair scores near zero instead of looking like a confident impostor.
ENC — one anchor
Only the batch mean is anchored and every individual norm stays free; that is what separates RipPLE from a fixed-norm baseline.

Watch the scores cross

Both faces of the genuine pair blur together while the impostor reference stays sharp. Scores are traced point by point, and at the heaviest blur the two meet.
